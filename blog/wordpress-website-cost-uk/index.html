How Much Does a WordPress Website Cost in the UK? (2026 Guide)

Premium DevelopersPublished 2 September 2025

Planning a WordPress website cost in the UK for 2026? Prices vary widely because “a WordPress site” can mean anything from a focused five-page brochure site to a WooCommerce store with custom checkout rules. This guide explains what you are paying for, typical budget ranges, and how to scope a project without nasty surprises.

What drives WordPress website pricing?

Most UK agencies and freelancers price around scope, not the WordPress software itself (which is free). The main cost drivers are:

  • Structure and content: number of templates, landing pages, blog setup, and who supplies copy
  • Design: bespoke layout versus a refined theme with Elementor or block editor
  • Functionality: forms, bookings, memberships, multilingual, or custom plugins
  • Ecommerce: WooCommerce catalogue size, shipping rules, tax, and payment gateways
  • Integrations: CRM, email marketing, analytics, and third-party APIs
  • Performance and SEO foundations: image workflow, caching, schema, and technical setup
  • Training and handover: documentation and editor training for your team

Typical UK budget ranges (indicative, not quotes)

These bands help you sanity-check proposals. Your final figure depends on requirements and who does the work (freelancer, agency, or in-house plus external build).

  • Essential business site (£1,500–£4,500): clear service pages, contact flows, mobile-responsive layout, basic SEO setup
  • Growth site (£4,500–£12,000): custom design system, multiple templates, blog, stronger performance work, integrations
  • WooCommerce store (£6,000–£25,000+): catalogue structure, checkout, payments, shipping, admin workflows, often phased delivery
  • Ongoing care (£50–£300+ per month): updates, backups, security monitoring, small content or dev tasks

VAT, content writing, photography, and premium plugins or licences are often quoted separately—clarify that upfront.

One-off build versus monthly retainers

Some businesses prefer a fixed project fee for launch, then a lighter maintenance retainer afterwards. Others need continuous improvements (new landing pages, CRO, catalog changes). Match the commercial model to how often the site must change.

Hidden costs to plan for

  • Hosting: managed WordPress hosting often saves time on speed and security
  • Licences: Elementor Pro, ACF, security tools, backup services
  • Payment fees: Stripe, PayPal, or merchant acquirer charges on top of platform fees
  • Migrations: moving from Wix, Squarespace, or an old WordPress install
  • Compliance: cookie consent, privacy policy alignment, accessibility improvements

How to get an accurate estimate

  1. List must-have pages and user journeys (e.g. “service → case study → contact”).
  2. Share reference sites you like—and what you dislike.
  3. State who provides copy, images, and product data.
  4. Call out integrations and deadlines (seasonal launches, exhibitions, funding milestones).
  5. Ask for phased delivery if budget is tight: launch core pages first, add ecommerce or extras later.

When professional WordPress development pays off

DIY or very low-cost builds can work for early-stage ideas. Professional development makes sense when the site is a revenue channel, when editors need a sustainable workflow, or when WooCommerce logic must be reliable day after day.
