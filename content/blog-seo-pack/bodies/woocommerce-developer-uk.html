What does a WooCommerce developer actually do?

A WooCommerce developer works inside WordPress to build or improve online stores. That includes product and category setup, cart and checkout behaviour, payment and shipping rules, and the integrations that connect your shop to accounting, email, or fulfilment tools.

  • Store architecture: categories, attributes, variations, and URLs that stay logical as the catalogue grows
  • Checkout: guest checkout, address fields, mobile layout, and fewer surprise steps for UK shoppers
  • Payments: Stripe, PayPal, Klarna, or other gateways matched to your markets
  • Shipping and tax: zones, rates, and VAT behaviour aligned with how you sell
  • Performance and security: sensible plugins, staging tests, and hosting that supports traffic
  • Handover: documentation so your team can manage routine updates

WooCommerce developer vs general WordPress developer

Many agencies build brochure WordPress sites well but treat WooCommerce as a plugin install. Store work needs someone comfortable with order flows, stock, refunds, and payment edge cases. Ask for examples of live shops, not only marketing sites.

When UK businesses hire a WooCommerce developer

  • Launching a first UK or international store on WordPress
  • Checkout abandonment or mobile conversion problems
  • Migrating from Shopify, Wix, or an outdated WooCommerce theme
  • B2B pricing, subscriptions, or complex shipping rules
  • Connecting ERP, Xero, or warehouse tools

How to choose a WooCommerce developer

Share your product count, target countries, payment methods, and any systems you must integrate. A practical partner will ask about fulfilment and support load, not only visual design. Request a scoped quote with phases rather than an open-ended hourly arrangement.
