WordPress vs Wix is a sensible first question for UK small businesses that need a professional site without enterprise complexity. Wix lowers the barrier to publish; WordPress trades a steeper setup for long-term flexibility.

Wix for UK small businesses

Strengths: fast start, managed hosting, predictable monthly pricing, and an editor many non-technical owners enjoy.

Trade-offs: advanced customisation, certain SEO scenarios, and complex integrations can hit platform limits. Moving away later requires a planned migration.

WordPress for UK small businesses

Strengths: full control of hosting (including UK/EU data location), unlimited layout options with Elementor or blocks, strong blogging and SEO tooling, and WooCommerce when you are ready to sell online.

Trade-offs: you own updates, security, and plugin choices—or you pay an agency for maintenance and support.

SEO and content growth

Both platforms can rank when content is useful and technical basics are sound. WordPress often wins when you need detailed URL control, custom post types, or content hubs that feed service and product pages. Wix has improved SEO tools, but compare redirect handling and blog structure if organic search is central to your plan.

Pricing lens

Wix’s subscription bundles hosting and builder. WordPress costs split across hosting, themes/builders, and development. Over three to five years, a modest WordPress site with maintenance can be competitive—especially if you avoid rebuilding when requirements grow.

Which should you choose?

  • Choose Wix if you need live quickly, sell simple services, and rarely change structure.
  • Choose WordPress if search, content marketing, WooCommerce, or custom integrations are part of your growth plan.
