WordPress website maintenance keeps your business site secure, fast, and editable. Small businesses often launch successfully then let updates slip—until a plugin conflict, hack attempt, or broken form surfaces during a busy week.

Weekly checks

  • Confirm contact and enquiry forms submit correctly
  • Review uptime or monitoring alerts
  • Check failed order or payment notifications (WooCommerce)

Monthly tasks

  • Apply WordPress core, theme, and plugin updates on staging first
  • Review user accounts; remove ex-staff administrators
  • Scan analytics for 404 spikes after changes
  • Verify backups completed and test a restore periodically

Quarterly tasks

  • Performance spot-check on mobile (PageSpeed or similar)
  • Review SSL certificate expiry and DNS records
  • Audit installed plugins; uninstall unused ones
  • Refresh critical page content and metadata

Security essentials

Use strong passwords and two-factor authentication for admins, limit login attempts, and choose reputable hosting. After updates, confirm cookie consent and tracking still behave as expected for UK visitors.

When to outsource maintenance

If nobody in the business owns updates, a WordPress maintenance and support plan is usually cheaper than emergency recovery. Pair maintenance with occasional performance reviews.
