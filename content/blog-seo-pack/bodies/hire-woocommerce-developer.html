When you hire a WooCommerce developer, you are buying more than theme tweaks—you need someone who understands ecommerce flows, security, and performance under real order volume.

Skills to look for

  • Custom checkout and cart behavior without breaking updates
  • Payment gateway configuration and test-mode validation
  • Responsive product and checkout layouts
  • Security hardening, backups, and update strategy
  • Performance optimization for catalog and checkout pages
  • Experience with shipping, tax, and multi-currency scenarios

How to evaluate portfolios

Look for live stores, not only marketing screenshots. Ask what the developer built vs. configured with plugins. Check mobile checkout, filter usability, and page speed on example sites.

Compare proposals fairly

Align scope documents: product count, migration, integrations, training, warranty period, and who owns accounts (hosting, Stripe, domain). The lowest quote often omits migration QA or launch support.

Common hiring mistakes

  • Choosing purely on hourly rate without written scope
  • Stacking conflicting plugins instead of solving root requirements
  • Skipping staging and payment test transactions
  • No plan for WooCommerce and WordPress updates after launch

Interview questions

Ask how they handle plugin conflicts, HPOS compatibility, checkout field changes, and rollback if an update breaks the store. Strong candidates explain trade-offs in plain language.
