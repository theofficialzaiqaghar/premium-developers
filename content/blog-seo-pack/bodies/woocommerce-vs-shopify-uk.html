For many UK retailers, the WooCommerce vs Shopify UK choice comes down to control versus convenience. Both can power serious online stores; the right platform depends on your team, budget, and how customised the buying journey must be.

Quick comparison for UK businesses

Costs UK owners should model

Shopify: plan fee, transaction fees (unless using Shopify Payments), apps, and theme costs. Budget for app creep as you add reviews, subscriptions, or B2B rules.

WooCommerce: hosting (often £25–£150+ per month for managed WordPress), premium extensions, developer time for custom features, and ongoing maintenance. Software licences are often lower than stacked Shopify apps for complex stores.

When WooCommerce fits UK stores

  • You publish lots of guides, case studies, or SEO content beside the shop
  • You need bespoke product logic, B2B pricing, or UK-specific shipping rules
  • You want full ownership of data and hosting within the UK or EEA where possible
  • You already run marketing on WordPress

When Shopify fits UK stores

  • You want the fastest standard store launch with less server management
  • Your team prefers an all-in-one admin without plugin conflicts
  • Your catalogue and checkout fit Shopify’s typical patterns

Make the decision with a roadmap

List year-one requirements: product count, subscriptions, marketplaces, POS, VAT MOSS, and integrations with accounting (Xero, QuickBooks). Run a short discovery with a developer before committing—platform changes are expensive mid-growth.
