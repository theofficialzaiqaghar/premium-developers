Elementor vs Divi is one of the most common questions for UK businesses building on WordPress. Both are visual page builders with large user bases; your choice should reflect editor workflow, performance budget, and who will maintain the site after launch.

Elementor at a glance

  • Works as a plugin with most themes; Elementor Hello theme is lightweight
  • Large widget library; Pro adds theme building, dynamic content, and forms
  • Familiar for freelancers and agencies; wide tutorial ecosystem
  • Performance depends on how many widgets, fonts, and animations each page loads

Divi at a glance

  • Theme + builder from Elegant Themes; visual editing across templates
  • Divi Cloud and layout packs speed up starting designs
  • Built-in A/B testing and role controls on higher plans
  • Can add weight if many modules load globally

Design flexibility

Both builders can produce polished marketing sites. Elementor often wins when teams already standardise on it across multiple client sites. Divi suits owners who want theme and builder from one vendor and like Divi’s layout library.

Performance and SEO

Neither builder is automatically “slow”—bloated pages are. Prefer reusable sections, limit font families, defer non-critical scripts, and use caching. For SEO, focus on semantic headings, internal links, and fast LCP on mobile; the builder brand matters less than implementation.

Editor experience for your team

Before you buy licences, run a one-page test: add a hero, service grid, and contact form. See which editor your marketing team finds intuitive. The best builder is the one your team will actually use correctly.
