What drives WooCommerce website pricing in the UK?

WooCommerce itself is free open-source software. You pay for the work and infrastructure around it. The cost of a WooCommerce website usually depends on:

  • Design: theme refinement versus custom storefront layouts (the main driver of WooCommerce website design cost)
  • Catalogue complexity: product count, variations, attributes, bulk import and SEO-friendly URLs
  • Checkout and payments: Stripe, PayPal, Apple Pay, subscriptions or B2B payment terms
  • Shipping and tax: UK/EU zones, live rates, click-and-collect, VAT rules
  • Integrations: CRM, accounting, email, inventory or ERP
  • Custom development: unique product flows, memberships or admin tools (custom WordPress development)
  • Content and data: who writes copy, photographs products and prepares CSV imports

Design cost versus development cost

WooCommerce website design cost covers visual system, product templates, cart/checkout UX and mobile layouts. Development covers WooCommerce configuration, plugins, payment/shipping setup, performance and security foundations. Many projects blend both; ask proposers to separate design hours from build hours so you can compare fairly.

Hosting, SSL and infrastructure

Budget monthly hosting separately from build fees. UK stores typically need:

  • Managed WordPress hosting suited to WooCommerce (CPU, object cache, staging)
  • SSL (usually included)
  • Automated backups and a staging environment for updates
  • Transactional email that reliably delivers order confirmations

Cheap shared hosting often becomes the most expensive choice when checkout fails under load.

Payment gateway and plugin costs

Payment providers charge per transaction (and sometimes monthly). Premium plugins (subscriptions, advanced shipping, page builders, security) add licence fees every year. List every paid plugin in your proposal so how much does WooCommerce cost to run is clear after launch—not only to build.

WooCommerce maintenance cost UK

Plan for ongoing care: WordPress core, WooCommerce and plugin updates, backups, uptime checks and small fixes. Typical retainers range from roughly £40–£300+ per month depending on store complexity and support hours. Major features and new templates are usually billed separately. How to get an accurate store quote

  1. Share approximate product count, variations and sample SKUs.
  2. List payment methods and shipping countries.
  3. Note must-have integrations and launch deadline.
  4. Confirm who supplies photos, descriptions and category structure.
  5. Ask for a phased plan if budget is tight: launch core catalogue first, then advanced features.
